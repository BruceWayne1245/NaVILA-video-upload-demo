# 2026-07-16 — Matching-primitive strategy: why the network survey's recommendation doesn't hold up, a partial 3D-height check, and the three candidate directions actually worth pursuing

**Purpose of this document**: `investigations/2026-07-15-50ep-batch-current-next-simultaneous-failure/low_density_lidar_matching_network_investigation.md` recommended a layered single-frame matching-primitive upgrade (bounded correlative SE(2) search + degeneracy-aware refinement + multi-hypothesis confidence). Before implementing any of it, this session cross-checked that recommendation against this project's own already-existing investigation history and found it does not hold up. This document records that finding, a follow-up partial 3D-structure check (with an important caveat about what it actually measured), and the three genuinely still-open candidate directions, in priority order, for whoever picks up the matching-primitive work next.

## Part 1 — the network survey's own priorities are already falsified by this project's prior work

The survey's Priority 1 (Hessian-eigenvalue directional degeneracy, reusing the already-logged `localizability` signal — X-ICP/DRPM-style) and Priority 2 (Cartographer-style bounded correlative SE(2) search) were both checked against real data already collected by this project, from two different sessions:

- **Priority 1 falsified this session (2026-07-16)**: ground-truth-checked `localizability.quality`/`min_normalized_eigenvalue`/`yaw_observability` against real current-role catastrophic (>45°) bearing errors across the 8 known-failure episodes of `shadow_hint_swap_50ep_20260714_accumulated` (n=4636 current-role readings). Result: these signals do **not** discriminate catastrophic from clean readings, and are **inverted** at some thresholds (catastrophic readings show `quality=full` 94.2% of the time vs. 67.7% for clean readings). Full detail: `investigations/2026-07-16-current-role-confidence-ambiguity-gate/CODE_CHANGE_current_confidence_gate.md` Part 1.
- **Priority 2 already falsified on 2026-07-13**, before the survey was even written: `investigations/2026-07-13-icp-bearing-error-cross-batch-deep-dive/GLOBAL_REGISTRATION_CHECK.md` ran a 360-seed (15x denser) search and a truth-seeded ICP initialization against real hard-anchor captures — found the wrong pose is a genuinely *better*-scoring optimum than the true pose under this project's own ICP metric, and a much denser search converges to the same (wrong) answer. `CORRELATIVE_VERIFIER_CHECK.md` then implemented the survey's exact Priority-2 mechanism (2D occupancy-grid FFT cross-correlation, a genuine joint (yaw,dx,dy) search, not a locally-refined one) against the same data and found no reliable improvement (9/16 and 6/16 readings beat ICP — coin-flip level).

**Both documents converge on the same structural conclusion, in their own words**: *"the ambiguity is genuine, repeated physical structure... that looks nearly identical to any generic 2D geometric overlap measure from a single viewpoint — not an artifact of one specific algorithm's scoring quirks"* and *"not recommended as further work in this line: additional single-frame scan-matching algorithms... absent a specific new reason to expect they'd behave differently."* This is not "two specific algorithms happened to fail" — it's a principled argument that generalizes to the entire class of single-frame, single-viewpoint 2D/near-2D point-set-overlap methods (which covers essentially everything the survey's Priority 3-5 also proposed: GenZ-ICP, ICET, NDT, GICP are all, at their core, the same category of measure). **A further broad literature survey is not warranted** — the remaining open questions are narrow, empirical, and implementation-focused, not "what does the literature say."

## Part 2 — partial 3D-height check: mixed result, with an important caveat about what was actually measured

Per the reasoning above (the shared blind spot is "single viewpoint," not "which 2D algorithm"), the next candidate to check was whether the vertical (height) dimension — already captured but currently discarded after a coarse pre-filter — contains real discriminating information current methods throw away.

**Method**: for the 16 real, previously-characterized confidently-wrong-yaw cases from `2026-07-13-.../GLOBAL_REGISTRATION_CHECK.md` (`ep367` anchor8, `ep368` anchor12, 8 steps each), computed both the true relative transform (from ground-truth world poses) and the ICP-preferred wrong transform (a real 24-seed sweep, same production function), applied both to each anchor's own raw captured 3D point cloud, and compared height (z) residuals against the current scan's 3D points under nearest-2D-neighbor correspondence — i.e. holding xy-alignment fixed, does z-alignment quality differ between the true and wrong transform?

**Result (14 cases with real >20° yaw error and both residuals computed)**: true-transform height residual clearly better in 6/14 (42.9%); roughly similar in 1/14; true-transform height residual clearly *worse* (ICP wins even in z) in 5/14 (35.7%). **A mixed, partial result — not a clean case for or against 3D-awareness.**

**Important caveat, found by direct inspection after the fact and worth stating explicitly so it isn't re-litigated**: the captured point clouds used for this check (`--capture_icp_replay_dataset`'s `local_map_points_xyz_body`) are **not** the complete original 3D LiDAR return — their z-range is `[-0.1999, 1.7989]`/`[-0.1998, 1.7581]` in the two spot-checked frames, matching almost exactly `_descriptor_local_map_points_raw`'s existing obstacle-band height filter (`z ∈ [-0.20, 1.80]`), applied **before** the capture was ever written. Whatever ceiling structure, high shelving, doorframe tops, etc. exist outside this ~2m band was already discarded upstream of the capture, not available to this check. **This check therefore only validated "does finer-grained z-residual matching within the band already used today add value" (a real, if partial, positive answer for ~43% of cases) — it does NOT answer "would genuinely richer 3D structure beyond the current band help,"** since that data doesn't exist in any current capture and would require either a new live Isaac Sim capture with the pre-filter removed/widened, or inspecting the `route_memory_lidar` sensor's own raw FOV directly.

## Part 3.5 — is the aliasing really "textureless corridors," or is it the anchor-capture methodology itself? (user-raised, checked directly against real data)

User recalled that `2026-07-15-.../FINDINGS.md` Part 7's turn-angle check found not all problem anchors sit on featureless corridor stretches — 2/7 (`ep367`, `ep498`) are directly at sharp turns (41-49°, 63.7°), contradicting a pure "it's just corridors" story. This raised the question: is the residual problem really about textureless environments, or about how anchors are built/matched regardless of the underlying geometry's distinctiveness?

**Directly re-verified** (`shadow_hint_swap_50ep_20260714_accumulated`, ground-truth anchor-to-anchor path geometry): confirmed `ep367` anchor5 (49.2°) and anchor8 (41.0°) are indeed at real, meaningfully sharp route-direction changes (anchor7, part of the same "5+7+8" bad group, is only 17.3° — more corridor-like).

**But their own saved local point clouds show `corridor_degeneracy_ratio` just as high as, or higher than, typical corridor stretches** — anchor5: 0.956, anchor7: 0.846, anchor8: 0.787 (median, pooled over all readings against that anchor) — far above the project's own historically-observed corridor floor (minimum ever seen: 0.403) and the corridor-skip threshold (0.15). **The turn objectively exists in the route's path geometry, but the anchor's own single instantaneous LiDAR capture — taken at whatever moment `anchor_spacing_m`'s cumulative-distance trigger happened to fire — does not actually see it.** This points at a capture-timing/positioning problem (a single frame's limited range/FOV, captured at one arbitrary instant, missing nearby distinguishing structure that objectively exists close by) rather than "no distinguishing information exists nearby at all."

**Follow-up check: how far away is the nearest real landmark from each known-bad anchor, in practice?** Walked anchor-to-anchor path-direction changes outward from each of the 10 known-bad anchors (5 episodes, `134/367/214/498/319`) to find the nearest genuine turn (>30°):

| episode/anchor | nearest turn | distance |
|---|---:|---:|
| `367`/5 | 49.2° | 0.00m |
| `367`/7 | 41.0° | 0.87m |
| `367`/8 | 41.0° | 0.00m |
| `498`/5 | 63.7° | 0.00m |
| `319`/4 | 48.3° | 0.82m |
| `319`/5 | 48.3° | 1.73m |
| `214`/3 | 61.0° | 5.26m |
| `214`/5 | 61.0° | 3.53m |
| `134`/7 | 179.3° | 3.29m |
| `214`/1 | — | not found within 7 anchors either side |

**9/10 have a genuine turn within 0-5.3m, most within 0-2m or at ~0m (i.e. very close, several essentially AT the anchor's own position)** — accumulating real motion over a modest, few-meter window should very plausibly capture a genuine landmark for most of these cases without needing an unbounded or very long reach. One exception (`ep214` anchor1) has no nearby turn found at all — a case Option 1 likely cannot fix regardless of window size, worth remembering as an expected residual, not a design failure.

**Net conclusion**: the residual problem is not best characterized as "this project's environments are fundamentally too textureless" — real, nearby distinguishing structure usually exists close to the failure points. It's better characterized as **a single-instantaneous-frame capture methodology (both at anchor-creation time and at live-match time) that is timing/position-sensitive and frequently fails to capture nearby distinguishing structure that does exist** — directly supporting Option 1 (motion-integrated accumulation) as addressing the actual mechanism, not just a differently-shaped guess.

## Part 4 — decision and priority (user-directed, 2026-07-16)

**Start with Option 1 (real motion-integrated multi-frame submap).** Rationale: it is the only one of the three that targets the actual shared root cause identified in Part 1 (the missing ingredient is information from a genuinely different viewpoint — real parallax across time/motion — not a better single-instant representation, 2D or 3D) rather than a different way of looking at the same single instant. It is also the option with the most legitimate historical caution attached — flagged as deferred since 2026-07-03 specifically because it needs real design decisions (anchor-creation timing, live-scan-matching design), not a parameter tweak — so it should be scoped and planned carefully before implementation begins, not treated as a quick fix.

**Fallback**: if Option 1 does not show a real improvement (checked against the same ground-truth methodology used throughout this investigation line — `P(current bad AND next bad)`, catastrophic->45° bearing-error rate, not just average accuracy), fall back to Option 2 (3D/height-aware matching within the existing captured band, or with a widened band if a new capture is warranted by then).

**Deferred, not next**: Option 3 (sensor retuning) — no offline path to test it, and it targets a fundamentally different problem (sparse/degenerate corridor geometry, the `corridor_or_sparse` bucket from earlier project history) rather than the aliasing problem this whole investigation line is about. Worth revisiting separately, on its own merits, not as part of this priority order.
