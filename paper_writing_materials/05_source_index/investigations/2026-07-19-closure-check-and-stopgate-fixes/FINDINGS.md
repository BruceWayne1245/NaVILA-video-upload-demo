## 0. Context

This session picked up mid-run on `canonical_report_next_50ep_20260719_accumulated` (the batch described in `investigations/2026-07-19-report-next-anchor-freezing-fix/FINDINGS.md`, launched earlier the same day). The goal shifted from validating the freezing fix in the abstract to forensically analyzing real return-phase failures as they accumulated, live, against the actual running batch's own data — using a novel technique introduced this session: importing `route_memory_agent.py`/`stop_gate.py` directly (both are pure Python + numpy, no Isaac Sim dependency) and replaying real recorded per-attempt data through the actual live decision functions, rather than inferring behavior from logs alone.

## 1. Methodology: ground-truth anchor-position reconstruction

`route_relocalization_diagnostics.covisibility_records` (present by default in every measurement JSON) logs every current/next ICP attempt's raw estimate, tagged with `anchor_distance_from_start_m` — the anchor's own *commanded*-arclength distance from the outbound start, fixed once at anchor-creation time. Anchors are created every `--route_anchor_spacing_m` (default 1.0m) of *commanded* distance traveled (`route_memory_agent.py::_should_save_anchor`), not real displacement.

To get each anchor's true world position: replayed the outbound trajectory's own `command` field (× `control_dt=0.02s`) to reconstruct the same cumulative-commanded-distance curve the live code uses to trigger anchor creation, matched it against each anchor's recorded distance, and read the trajectory's `position` field (Isaac's privileged ground truth, used here for verification/analysis only, never fed into the live non-oracle pipeline) at the matching step. Verified to 0.000m match error against the anchor-creation trigger logic on every episode checked. This is the basis for every "true distance" / "true position" claim below.

**Important, repeated methodology lesson this session**: an anchor's *true* separation from a neighbor is not always close to the nominal 1.0m spacing — some anchor pairs were reconstructed only ~0.1m apart in reality despite being ~1.0m apart in the commanded-distance bookkeeping (see §3). Also, enumerating anchors from `covisibility_records` alone only shows anchors that were actually attempted as a current/next candidate during *return* — outbound creates every anchor unconditionally regardless of what happens during return, so a first pass that inferred "this episode never had anchor 2" from `covisibility_records` alone was wrong and had to be corrected by replaying the anchor-creation trigger over the *full* outbound trajectory instead (see §5).

## 3. `ep4`/`ep319`: NOT a hint/anchor/VLM-decision failure — terrain/physical-contact-drift during locomotion (with corrections along the way)

**This conclusion went through two wrong intermediate framings before landing on the right one — documented here because the corrections are as informative as the final answer.**

**Wrong framing #1**: initial pass characterized `ep319`'s repeated near-identical hedge-text hint ("position uncertain...") as a literal frozen/cached value, blaming a gap in the same-day `suppress_if_stale` fix. **This was wrong.** Checking the *raw ground-truth trajectory* (`position` field, not route-memory's own odometry-derived pose) showed the robot's true world position was **completely stationary** (`[0.981, -8.023]`, speed≈0.006 m/s noise floor) for the entire ~540-step window. The reported values showed small, genuine step-to-step variation (not literal caching) — they converge to nearly the same reading because that reading is *correct*: a stationary robot really does look the same from ICP's perspective every time. Lesson: near-identical repeated readings are consistent with *either* a caching bug *or* a genuinely static physical state — check the raw ground-truth position/velocity trace before concluding "frozen cache."

**Wrong framing #2**: having found the robot was physically stationary, the next framing was "hint accurate, `hint_action_arbiter` detected the conflict but its own clear-path gate refused to act — a passivity gap in the arbiter's design." **This was also incomplete** (and was already a previously-known limitation the user had identified before this session, not new — see the note on precise language below). Tracing `ep4`'s *fine-grained* (every-step, not the every-25-step sampling used for aggregate accuracy stats) `command`/`yaw_deg` history found the real mechanism one level deeper: from return-start (`yaw=-103.6°`) to settling (`yaw=-71.8°`), most of the ~32° net yaw change happened during 150+ consecutive steps of `cmd=[0.5,0,0]` (**pure forward, zero commanded rotation** — e.g. steps 1650-1800, yaw drifted -104.3°→-78.1° with no rotation input at all), while explicit turn-right commands (matching the hint's own suggested direction) produced much smaller effects (~2.6° per 25 steps). This is the signature of a legged robot's controller passively rotating from contact forces while pushing into an angled obstacle — not a navigation-stack decision at all. `ep319` shows the same core mechanism as an oscillating tug-of-war rather than a monotonic drift: repeated forward-only windows each induce passive drift in the same rotational direction while interspersed turn-right commands partially counteract it, netting <0.15m position change over 300+ steps.

The anchor targeted (`ep4`'s anchor 8) was independently confirmed to be the geometrically correct, on-route anchor (ground-truth position lies exactly on the true outbound corridor at the correct arc-length) and the hint's reported bearing to it was numerically accurate throughout (99.3% of readings within 0.5m/30° of ground truth). **Conclusion**: this class of "stuck in a corner" failure is attributable to terrain/physical contact dynamics during locomotion, not to hint content, anchor selection, or VLM decision-making — none of the navigation decision layers meaningfully caused or could have prevented the specific rotation that led to the stall, since it happens as a passive side-effect of executing a "move forward" command near an obstacle.

**A note on precise language, since it recurred twice in this session**: describing `hint_action_arbiter`'s clear-path gating as "designed to never act" overstated a known, pre-existing limitation as if it were a fresh discovery, which cost real time re-confirming something already understood. The corrected framing: the arbiter's conflict *detection* worked correctly; the *override* was declined by a separate, already-known-strict clear-path check. Precision matters here — state exactly which check fired and why, not a one-sentence summary implying more than was verified.

## 4. `closure_check` was silently starving `sequential_pair`'s own promotion mechanism

**Root question**: why did `sequential_pair`'s bounded-evidence/quarantine/alias-aware promotion gates — specifically designed to prevent a single anchor from getting stuck as "next" — fail to resolve `ep5`'s anchor pair (13,12), which never promoted across the *entire* 1001-attempt return phase?

**Answer, verified by direct code replay (not log inference)**: `_sequential_pair_closure_precheck` (the current/next cross-validation check, re-enabled this same day per `CANONICAL_CONFIG.md`) was rejecting the vast majority of attempts, and its rejection caused `_select_sequential_pair_relocalization` to **return immediately, before the promotion-vote code ever ran** — not even recording a failed vote.

| Episode | Anchor pair | Reject rate (`dtheta`, live config) | True anchor separation |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `ep5` | (13, 12) | **998/1001 = 99.7%** | ~0.1m (vs. ~1.0m nominal spacing) |
| `ep678` | (17, 16) | **412/439 = 93.8%** | ~0.1m |
| `ep187` | (12, 11) | **74/120 = 61.7%** | ~1.05m (normal) |
| `ep589` | (12, 11) / (13, 12) | **220/238=92.4% / 328/335=97.9%** | ~0.83m (normal) |

**Root mechanism for the near-0.1m-apart pairs**: `dtheta` (ICP-estimated rotation) disagreement between the two sides was large and *stable* (`ep5`: ~124-136° the whole episode, not random noise) while **position disagreement stayed small** (0.16-0.24m, well under the 0.75m threshold) throughout. Two near-identical, low-parallax local point clouds are easy for ICP to alias into different rotational solutions even when translation stays accurate. `dtheta` is the *same* variable this project's own 2026-07-13 investigation already found unreliable (median 38° error vs. `bearing`'s 2.2°, r≈0.40 correlation on the same readings) — but that investigation's fix only touched the *other* `dtheta` consumer (`_anchor_progress_from_estimate`'s hint-text generation, fixed 07-14); `sequential_pair_closure_reconciliation_signal` (which governs exactly this cross-check) was left at its `dtheta` default the whole time, an incomplete rollout rather than a deliberate choice.

**Verified the fix by replaying the same recorded attempts with `reconciliation_signal="bearing"`**: reject rate dropped to 30.5% (`ep5`) and 30.3% (`ep678`). **`ep187`/`ep589` showed no improvement whatsoever** with `bearing` (`ep187`: identical 74/120; `ep589`: 221/238 vs 220/238, no meaningful change) — confirmed their rejections are driven by genuine **position** disagreement (116/120 attempts exceed 0.75m for `ep187`), not a `dtheta` artifact. This is a separate, still-open problem the signal switch does not address.

**A second, independent bug, found by asking why reconstruction (the mechanism's "fix the weaker side from the stronger" fallback) wasn't firing instead of outright rejecting**: reconstruction only triggers when one side's quality (`confidence*sqrt(inlier_count)`) dominates the other by >1.5x. For `ep5`, the quality ratio exceeded 1.5 on only 3/1001 (0.3%) attempts (median 1.03) — the two near-co-located anchors naturally produce near-identical ICP quality scores, so the code can essentially never tell "which side to trust." Precisely classified `ep187`'s 120 attempts: 4 clean pass, 42 reconstructed, **74 outright-rejected with no reconstruction and (previously) no bookkeeping trace at all** — a genuine structural gap between the mechanism's only two designed outcomes (agree, or reconstruct), with nothing for "real disagreement, quality tied."

## 5. Fix #1 (code, unconditional): closure-mismatch now counts as an explicit failed vote

`route_memory_agent.py::_select_sequential_pair_relocalization` previously `return`ed immediately on `closure_reject_reason`, skipping the promotion-vote window *and* `promotion_alias_stall_attempts`'s own counter for the whole attempt — so a persistently-mismatching pair left zero record of the attempt happening, and the 200-attempt stall-relief valve (meant to fall back to a more lenient threshold on a persistently-stuck candidate) never advanced its counter on rejected attempts either.

**Fixed to mirror `sequential_pair_short_baseline_require_resolution`'s own already-established pattern** (run promotion bookkeeping normally, override only the final decision) instead of dropping the attempt from history: `closure_reject_reason` is now deferred and applied as a forced `candidate_promote=False` right where the vote gets cast, so `bounded_evidence`'s window and the stall counter both now see and count every closure-mismatched attempt as an explicit "no". **Unconditional — no new flag, always active** for the `sequential_pair` backend. Verified: 280/280 pre-existing tests still pass, zero regressions.

## 7. `stop_gate`'s "deferred"≈"pass" gap — confirmed on 4 episodes, not fixed by §5/§6

Independently of the closure_check issue, `ep187`/`ep498`/`ep589`/`ep678` all show the *same* mechanism causing a premature-stop acceptance: `stop_gate.py::ReturnStopGate.check()` returns `"deferred"` (not `"vetoed"`) whenever `conf < min_confidence` at the moment the VLM issues a stop — and the caller (`round_trip_eval.py`'s stop-handling block) only special-cases `"vetoed"`/`"forced"`; `"deferred"` falls through exactly like `"pass"`, so the stop is accepted unchallenged.

| Episode | Confidence at the accepted stop | Distance at the accepted stop | Current anchor's own `anchor_route_remaining_m` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `ep187` | 0.495 | 13.40m | 11.09m |
| `ep678` | 0.493 | 13.85m | 12.10m |
| `ep498` | 0.496 | 4.79m | 3.01m (borderline) |
| `ep589` | 0.412 | 13.63m | 11.09m |

`ep498` was confirmed to have **zero** `closure_check` rejections on its dominant anchor pairs (clean promotion the whole episode) — its failure is purely the `stop_gate` gap, unrelated to §4-§6. `ep589` has *both* problems (anchor pair stuck per §4, AND the same `stop_gate` gap at the end) — and its anchor pair's rejections are the genuine-position-disagreement flavor (like `ep187`), not fixed by the `bearing` switch.

In every one of `stop_gate`'s two immediately-preceding checks (2-3 attempts earlier), the gate correctly `vetoed` the same episode's earlier premature-stop attempts while confidence was still ≥0.5 — the gate was working as designed right up until confidence dipped a few hundredths below the threshold at the critical attempt.

## 8. Anchor-index-to-true-distance statistics (all 8 outbound-success episodes)

Requested to evaluate a proposed fix: use the route-memory anchor currently tracked as "current" as an independent signal for stop/no-stop authority. First pass (wrong, see the methodology note in §1) used only `covisibility_records`' anchor set and found anchor indices inconsistent across episodes. **Corrected by replaying the full outbound anchor-creation trigger** (§1): anchor 2 is within 3.0m of the true start in **all 8** episodes; anchor 3 in 7/8. Anchor identity itself is NOT a stable cross-episode index-to-distance mapping in the way `covisibility_records` alone suggested — but each anchor's own recorded distance-from-start (`anchor_route_remaining_m`, already available non-oracle) is a per-episode, per-anchor, reliable-enough structural signal.

**A real risk was flagged and had to be designed around**: `ep319`'s current anchor was `anchor 2` (a "close" anchor) at the moment it got physically stuck (§3), yet its final true distance from start was 3.74m — anchor identity had gone stale after the robot promoted to a close anchor and then physically drifted away from it (terrain dynamics). Anchor identity alone is not sufficient corroboration for a forced stop; it must agree with the current (otherwise low-confidence) reading, and ideally over a short streak of consecutive attempts, not a single one.

## 9. Fix #3 (new mechanism, flag): anchor-corroborated stop-gate authority

Implemented in `stop_gate.py` (see file for full design rationale in the module docstring), off by default via `anchor_corroboration_enabled` (CLI: `--stop_gate_anchor_corroboration`):

- **Veto direction**: at low confidence, if the current anchor's own `anchor_route_remaining_m` is not itself a "close" one (`> r_in`) AND the current (otherwise-untrusted) reading also says far (`d > r_out`), veto regardless of confidence. Validated directly against all 4 confirmed §7 episodes: 3/4 (`ep187`, `ep589`, `ep678`) have an anchor `anchor_route_remaining_m` of 11-12m, decisively far on its own; `ep498`'s borderline anchor (3.01m) combined with its own reading (4.79m) also clears the bar. If anchor and reading disagree, stays `deferred` rather than guessing.
- **Forced-stop direction**: requires the anchor's own distance AND the current reading to *both* say "home" for `forced_stop_anchor_confirm_steps` (default 2) *consecutive* attempts, not a single one — directly guards against the `ep319`-style stale-anchor-after-drift risk from §8. A single disagreeing attempt (anchor OR reading) resets the streak, as does a teleport frame.

`test_stop_gate.py`: 15 new tests added (8 veto-side, 7 forced-side), built directly from the `ep187`/`ep498` numeric shapes above plus contradiction/off-by-default/teleport-reset edge cases. Full suite: 295/295 pass (280 pre-existing + 15 new), zero regressions.
