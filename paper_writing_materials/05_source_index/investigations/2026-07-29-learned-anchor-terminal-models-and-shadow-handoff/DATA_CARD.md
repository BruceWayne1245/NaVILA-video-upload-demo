Only 28.2% of rows contain the oracle next anchor among the anchors actually
probed by the historical controller. This is an important policy-coverage
fact, not a label failure:

- the dataset is immediately suitable for transition/rebase detection and
  sequence-state training;
- a full posterior over every saved anchor requires an additional offline ICP
  replay that evaluates a wider candidate set;
- rows with `oracle_next_present_in_candidates=false` must not be treated as
  ordinary closed-set candidate-classification negatives.

## Recommended first training pass

Anchor model:

1. train on rows with `sample_weight > 0`;
2. predict oracle next-anchor offset and a separate `needs_rebase` target;
3. treat candidate selection as open-set when the oracle target is absent;
4. evaluate by scene, episode, and contiguous dwell, not by randomly shuffled
   rows;
5. retain V1.1 probabilities as observation-quality features.

Terminal model:

1. train a three-way calibrated classifier for `arrived/boundary/far`;
2. use class weighting or balanced sampling for `boundary`;
3. report false-arrived rate separately and treat it as the primary safety
   metric;
4. keep historical stop-gate decisions out of model inputs;
5. convert probabilities to accept/reject/verify only in an external policy
   layer.

## Known limitations

- 127 episodes predate exact attempt-step logging and use explicit half-weight
  interpolation; 155 use exact V1.1 step metadata.
- Historical trajectories were generated by older policies, so active-model
  deployment will create policy distribution shift.
- Labels use saved anchor world poses and are supervision-only.
- Low-quality/off-route rows are retained at zero weight for audit and future
  robust/open-set work.
- The dataset contains scalar/geometric diagnostics, not raw RGB/depth tensors.
  Raw capture paths are available through `episodes.jsonl`.
