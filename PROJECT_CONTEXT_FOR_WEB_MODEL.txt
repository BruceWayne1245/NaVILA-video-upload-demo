PURPOSE
=======
This project reproduces NaVILA (RSS 2025) in Isaac Sim for VLN-CE-style indoor
navigation on an RTX 4090 workstation. The robot first follows a route using
the VLM and records route anchors. It then attempts to return to the start by
relocalizing against those anchors. The research focus has shifted from merely
making the benchmark run to making return navigation reliable, diagnosable,
and safe under perceptual aliasing and runtime failures.

CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTION
=========================
How can a VLM-guided robot perform reliable closed-loop route return when
LiDAR/ICP localization can be geometrically plausible yet confidently wrong in
repetitive indoor scenes, and when downstream anchor, hint, and terminal-stop
decisions can amplify such errors?

SYSTEM PIPELINE
==============
The current design is:

  ICP / A0 / VLM / action-integrated motion
              |
              v
  Reliability V1.1 + dependency health
       pose / bearing / distance authority
              |
              v
  Temporal Route Belief
  anchor/home posterior, evidence age, contradictions, motion, OOD
         /             |              \
        v              v               v
  Anchor V2       Hint split       Terminal sequence
   active          models             shadow
              |
              v
  deterministic safety executor

Authority boundaries are important: V1.1 is the only ICP-derived reliability
authority. Pose may authorize Anchor evidence, bearing may authorize Hint
evidence, and distance may authorize Terminal evidence. Raw ICP quality or
confidence must not bypass V1.1. Terminal STOP authority remains deterministic
until a learned sequential Terminal model demonstrates both safety and useful
recall.

MAIN TECHNICAL FINDINGS
=======================
1. Indoor geometry contains severe perceptual/rotational aliasing. Similar
   corridors, junctions, doorways, and rooms can produce multiple ICP poses
   with similarly good geometric scores.
2. Many return failures are confidently-wrong-driven rather than simple
   low-confidence failures. Scalar features such as inlier count, residual,
   score ratio, and localizability do not reliably separate the wrong basin.
3. A July fusion investigation showed that sequential current/next belief
   fusion could corrupt an already-correct ICP reading when two poses had a
   large near-antipodal disagreement. A trust-aware guard reconstructs from the
   trustworthy side when match_class and near-tie evidence identify exactly
   one trustworthy side. It improved the ep187 anchor14 offline mean bearing
   error from 48.30 degrees to 8.91 degrees, but does not solve two mutually
   consistent, clean-looking wrong estimates.
4. In a broad hard-11 survey, raw ICP was often better than fused output; the
   residual problem is a mixture of fusion-induced corruption and genuine raw
   ICP aliasing. The latter is the harder open problem.
5. Only a small fraction of wrong ICP attempts have the true answer among the
   persisted top-four candidates (reported ceiling about 4.8%). Re-ranking
   top-four candidates alone is therefore insufficient unless the candidate
   search is widened or changed.
6. Tested alternatives include scalar reliability classifiers, nearby-view
   spread, motion-integrated multi-frame submaps, static outbound-heading
   priors, and multi-anchor consistency. They were rejected or found
   insufficient for the main symmetric-scene failure. RGB/LoFTR visual
   disambiguation and wider ICP hypothesis logging/search remain promising
   research directions.

EVIDENCE AND NUMBERS TO HANDLE CAREFULLY
========================================
- The early README reports an end-to-end Episode 0 result of success=1.0 and
  SPL=0.907, and an early corrected round-trip result of 8/11. These are early
  snapshots, not the final project result.
- The July 22 best-result package reports a 63% baseline; consult its README,
  per_episode_truth.tsv, and the associated reliability reports for exact
  cohort definitions.
- A July 23 prospective 100-episode evidence package supports reliability and
  control-readiness claims. Keep shadow effects separate from navigation
  effects.
- The July 30 Unified50 run was an evaluation/shadow collection with no
  controller effect. Its learned consumers should not be described as active
  navigation improvements.
- The earlier August 1 Route 2 V1.1-off collection was canceled/superseded.
  The corrected Core architecture and its development24/locked-validation20
  protocol are the authoritative later direction.
- The August 2 Anchor V2 full-active implementation passed 101/101 local
  tests. One-step held-out results reported promotion precision of 0.9971 on
  EU6 validation, 0.9850 on x8 test, and 0.9969 on locked20; locked20 recall
  was 0.3691. These numbers describe controller-state decisions, not full
  navigation success.
- The August 2 ep368 smoke completed outbound, return, and round trip, with
  348 active directives, 17 promotions, 22 bounded recoveries, and 9
  same-anchor holds. This is a wiring smoke, not a statistically sufficient
  deployment result.

CURRENT STATUS AS OF THE LATEST DOCUMENTS
=========================================
The project is not finished. Anchor V2 is the current active-development
stage. Its full-active controller has been implemented and smoke-tested, and a
larger serial batch is planned/queued behind a predecessor run. Temporal Route
Belief, Hint reconstruction, and Terminal reconstruction are intentionally not
being activated together. The approved order is:

  0. infrastructure, supervision, provenance, and rollback
  1. Anchor V2 full-active
  2. Temporal Route Belief
  3. Hint split/retraining
  4. Terminal sequence model, activated last

Every phase must preserve a baseline/control, use causal replay, keep locked
cohorts evaluation-only, report per-scene worst cases, and distinguish model
effects from infrastructure failures. The paper should therefore present the
work as an evolving reliability and safety architecture with completed
diagnostic findings plus ongoing validation, not as a finished benchmark
solution.

IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS
=====================
- No online oracle/ground-truth position may be used.
- Long-horizon accumulated odometry was rejected; bounded short-horizon motion
  remains an open question.
- Some episodes fail because the robot physically stops or because VLM/runtime
  infrastructure fails. These must not be silently merged with localization
  failures.
- Time-stamped live-run status files describe historical snapshots. They do not
  prove that a service is still running on the current host.
- Do not use locked-validation20 to tune thresholds, select features, or fit
  models.
- Do not claim that shadow-only models improved navigation; shadow means their
  decisions did not control the robot.

SUGGESTED PAPER STRUCTURE
=========================
1. Introduction: VLM navigation is capable but closed-loop return is fragile
   under perceptual aliasing.
2. Problem formulation and system setup: NaVILA, Isaac Sim, route anchors,
   return phase, ICP and current/next closure.
3. Failure analysis: confidently-wrong ICP, fusion corruption, promotion and
   terminal-stop amplification, and infrastructure confounds.
4. Reliability architecture: V1.1 authority, evidence gating, shadow/control
   protocol, provenance, and causal replay.
5. Route 2 controller design: temporal belief, Anchor V2, Hint split, and
   Terminal sequence safety boundary.
6. Experimental protocol: historical replay, development24, locked20,
   prospective cohorts, shadow versus active, and failure taxonomy.
7. Results available so far: early baseline, bearing-guard result, reliability
   evidence, Anchor V2 offline/controller metrics, and ep368 smoke.
8. Limitations and future work: RGB/LoFTR, wider hypothesis search, unresolved
   information limits, physical stuck behavior, and unfinished live validation.

For every numerical statement in the paper, cite the exact source file in
paper_writing_materials/04_key_data_and_reports/ or the corresponding dated
investigation. Preserve cohort names, controller versions, and whether a run
was shadow, active, canceled, infrastructure-invalid, or evaluation-only.
